The Mechanism of Bias

Standard sensors use Red and Infrared light. However, higher levels of melanin absorb this light differently, confusing the algorithm into misinterpreting melanin absorption as oxygenated blood, creating a dangerous overestimation.

The Perfusion Factor

Melanin isn't the only variable. When a patient has low Perfusion (PI), meaning weak blood flow due to cold hands or shock, the physical optical signal becomes inherently weak. Combined with high melanin blocking the light, the hardware relies heavily on faulty algorithmic assumptions.

Limits of Software Correction

By toggling the "Correction Algorithm," the simulator applies hypothetical dynamic offsets to mitigate the bias. Notice that it is not perfect. Software cannot perfectly reconstruct a signal that never reached the physical sensor. In extreme cases (High MST + Low PI), residual bias will still exist.

Core Metrics

  • SpO2 (Device Display): The estimated oxygen saturation calculated by the optical sensor. What the doctor sees.
  • SaO2 (True Arterial Blood): The actual, physical oxygen level in the bloodstream.
  • PI (Perfusion Index): The strength of the pulsatile blood flow. Lower PI means weaker physical signals.
  • MST (Monk Skin Tone): A 10-point scale used by researchers to benchmark optical inclusivity.
